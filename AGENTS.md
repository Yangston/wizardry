# Wizardry project decisions

- The user's confirmed touch-free entry is wrist wake, then an AssistiveTouch **double finger touch** assigned to **Activate Wizardry**. The shortcut already arms after hold-still calibration. Do not reintroduce an additional twist-to-wake mechanism.
- AssistiveTouch single finger touch is assigned to **None**. Wizardry owns custom, personalized single-touch recognition and routes the input according to interaction context. Its first action is locking live computer volume.
- Volume entry triggers immediately when absolute wrapped z / yaw reaches **55 degrees** from the looking-at-watch pose captured at the ready haptic. Either direction qualifies; there is no upper-angle cutoff or additional stillness delay. A face-normal comparison misses pure z rotation. Once adjusting, crossing the viewing yaw must not exit volume. Adjustment is now a **signed wrist-twist knob**, replacing vertical translation: positive roll increases, negative roll decreases, and 180 degrees spans the full volume range. Anchor at actual output volume on entry, wrap angle deltas, clamp without windup, and rebase during explicit sensing/tap freezes instead of replaying hidden movement.
- The display must stay awake during backward tilt, movement, and stationary pauses in live volume. Dimming with continued processing does not meet this requirement. Acquire temporary autorotation when foreground calibration starts and retain the same bounded lease through readiness, volume, and the final acknowledgement. Its ten-minute cap starts at calibration; inactive calibration may continue only with the lease and fresh samples, while backgrounding cancels it. The user reported rotation working in build 15.1; build 16.1 stationary-hold behavior and broader physical acceptance remain unverified. Do not promise an OS guarantee.
- Holding still must not automatically end live volume or release autorotation. Preserve manual lock, sensing/transport failures, explicit interruptions, and the ten-minute cap. The one-second unchanged-volume heartbeat keeps a healthy stationary session alive; six-second backend missing-request watchdogs remain enforced and must not be confused with a motion-inactivity timeout.
- Smooth live volume uses transport v2: up to 50 update starts per second, at most four outstanding Watch updates, and a priority final end. The phone serializes one executing write and one newest waiting target; superseded replies are not volume acknowledgements. The receiver uses a 50/s token bucket with burst capacity four. Require updated phone/receiver capabilities and retain bounded, credential-free diagnostics; confirmed acknowledgement Hz is not measured applied-output Hz.
- Keep existing discrete gesture mappings. Extension has priority; volume mode suppresses those actions until fresh activation.
- Watch Computer/Phone connection controls select one command target at a time, always through the paired iPhone. Disconnect stops Wizardry control without unpairing devices or deleting saved receiver credentials. Studio monitoring is independent of command targeting and requires explicit Computer studio enablement on the foreground iPhone.
- Desktop named movement recording must wait for Watch confirmation before prompting movement, disable gesture actions while capturing, retain quality/interruption evidence, and save only confirmed recording samples. Named recordings and custom mappings are preparation for later training, not an installed or validated recognizer. Keep the Watch app visible; do not claim background capture.
- The iPhone volume bridge uses an ordinary MPVolumeView rather than subclassing the unsupported native view, and still requires actual system-volume readback. Knob control, finger-touch recognition, and the native-slider bridge remain experimental. CI/synthetic tests do not establish physical-watch accuracy, suspension behavior, latency, or battery life.
- Preserve immediate delivery, expiry, session ordering, dry runs, pairing-token privacy, and no automatic command retries. No artificial workouts or silent audio for background runtime.
